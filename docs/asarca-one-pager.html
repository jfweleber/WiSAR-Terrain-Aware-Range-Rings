WiSAR Terrain-Aware Range Rings

Range rings that follow the ground. Statistical find distances are traced across real terrain, compressing against cliffs, forest and water and stretching along trails and drainages, with an overlay showing where subjects have historically been found.

What the tool does

Drop an initial planning point, pick a subject profile, and in well under a minute you have containment rings on the map that respect the terrain a subject actually had to cross.

  1. Builds a friction surface. Every 30 m cell gets a travel cost from land cover (Annual NLCD 2024, calibrated to off-trail speed literature). Trails, roads and power-line corridors from OpenStreetMap are burned in as easy travel; lakes, rivers and streams from USGS NHDPlus High Resolution become barriers.
  2. Runs cost-distance from the IPP. Dijkstra’s algorithm over a 1/3 arc-second USGS 3DEP elevation model, with Tobler’s hiking function applied per step so uphill, downhill and side-hill each cost what they should, plus true 3D distance.
  3. Contours the result. Either at Lost Person Behavior find-distance percentiles (TARR mode) or at hours of travel for a chosen speed (Travel Time mode).

Terrain attractor priority: Jacobs’s PDEN

A ring answers how far. It says nothing about where inside it to look first. In 2015 Matt Jacobs, working from ISRID find locations in Oregon, New York and Arizona (mostly hikers and hunters), asked how much more often subjects turn up near a terrain feature than that feature’s share of the search area would predict. That ratio is the probability density, PDEN. A PDEN of 1 means finds land there no more often than chance. A PDEN of 10 means ten times more often.

How the overlay is drawn

  • Each cell takes the strongest applicable attractor, not the sum. Jacobs’s categories overlap, and adding them would double-count a trail that follows a creek.
  • Color runs from cool (no attractor) to hot (a stream–trail intersection). Warm cells are where to send the first team inside a ring. They do not move the ring.
  • The overlay covers the whole search area, past the 75% ring. About one find in four falls outside the 75th percentile (Koester), and Jacobs found that linear-feature PDEN rises with distance from the IPP.
  • It is an optional layer, off by default: Map Layers › Terrain Attractor Priority.

What it is not

The overlay shows where finds have clustered historically. It does not feed the friction surface, the rings, or any probability-of-area figure; the ring math is unchanged whether it is on or off. Jacobs’s multipliers come from three states’ data, so the tool uses them to decide what to highlight and keeps the magnitudes conservative.

Into the field

Push rings straight to a CalTopo map as named shapes (any team, using its own CalTopo credentials), or export KML and GeoJSON for CalTopo, Google Earth, TAK, QGIS and Avenza. PNG overlays and GeoTIFF rasters are available for GIS work.
